How much baseline data do I need?

There is no universal minimum number of months. Collect enough data to represent the conditions that affect energy use and to estimate savings with the precision your decision requires.

Use these three steps to set and explain the amount of data. The final plan should state the savings calculation, the conditions it must represent, and the acceptable uncertainty.

A simple planning method

1Define the decision

Write down what savings will be calculated, what operating conditions affect it (such as weather, production, or product mix), and how precise the result needs to be.

2Cover those conditions

Collect data across the seasons, products, operating modes, and complete process or storage cycles that matter to the savings claim.

Seasonal project: a full year is a sound starting point. Extend it if the year was unusual or missed important conditions.

3Check precision and gaps

Estimate uncertainty using the project’s analysis method. Allow for related observations, different operating groups, missing data, and unusual periods.

If uncertainty is too large or important conditions are missing, collect more data, extend the period, or report the limitation.

How to explain the answer: “We collected [periods / cycles] to cover [important seasons and operating conditions]. This supports [savings calculation] at [precision or uncertainty]. We checked [gaps, related observations, and unusual conditions].”

Apply the method to all five Team A examples

Season + process cycles

SVC / Armstrong · Process heat-recovery heat pump

Start with a full year when weather or heat demand changes by season. Measure source and delivered heat, compressor and auxiliary electricity, steam or fuel displaced, and complete operating modes.

Complete production cycles

Glidewell · Electric ladle preheater

Count complete preheat and pour cycles across ladle sizes, products, shifts, and target temperatures. Record gas or electricity, tonnage, warm-up time, temperature, and quality.

Complete charge / discharge cycles

Element 16 · Thermal energy storage

Measure complete charge-to-discharge cycles, including standby and losses. Keep delivered heat and electricity as separate measures; cover dispatch modes and relevant seasons.

Time periods + operating cycles

JoulesAI / Manus Bio · Battery control

Compare control strategies over representative tariff periods, seasons, and dispatch modes. Align site load, prices, battery charge/discharge, state of charge, and operating limits.

Complete production cycles

CEIA / Donaldson · Induction curing

Count complete cure events across product types and operating modes. Measure generator, controls, and chiller energy; link each event to accepted output, defects, idle time, and downtime.
